TEKS C.5C · Periodic trends: electronegativity, ionization energy, atomic radius

Background: the "upper" trends

Open each term and read it. The bold line is the idea to remember.

Effective nuclear charge (Zeff)

Effective nuclear charge is the net positive pull from the nucleus's protons on the electrons. Inner (core) electrons shield the valence electrons from feeling the full pull of the protons.

Zeff increases across a period because protons are added. Zeff is reduced down a family because additional energy levels put the valence electrons farther from the nucleus.

Across a period: Zeff goes up. Down a family: the pull felt by valence electrons goes down.

Shielding

Shielding occurs when the innermost (core) electrons block valence electrons from the full pull of the protons in the nucleus.

More energy levels means more core electrons and more shielding.

Electron-electron repulsion

Electrons are all negatively charged, so they push away from each other. This repulsion makes the electron cloud expand. The effect can influence electronegativity and ionization energy, particularly in p-block elements.

Repulsion between electrons spreads the cloud out.

Atomic radius

Atomic radius is the distance from the nucleus to the edge of the electron cloud. It is measured as half the distance between two identical, bonded nuclei.

Across a period, radius increases from right to left: Zeff decreases while shielding stays the same, so fewer protons pull less strongly on the same energy levels and the elements on the left are larger. Down a family, radius increases because of added energy levels.

Radius increases toward the left and toward the bottom of the table.

Atomic mass

Atomic mass increases from left to right across a period because of increasing numbers of protons (atomic number) and, typically, neutrons. It also increases down a family for the same reason.

Atomic mass generally increases toward the right and toward the bottom.

Ionization energy

Ionization energy is the energy required to remove the highest-energy electron from a neutral atom in the gas state.

It increases across a period (left to right) because greater Zeff pulls the cloud closer, so more energy is needed to remove an electron. It increases from the bottom of a family to the top because there are fewer energy levels between the valence electrons and the nucleus.

Ionization energy increases toward the right and toward the top.

Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the ability of an atom to attract electrons in a chemical bond. It increases across a period and from the bottom of a family to the top.

On the left (metals), valence shells are less than half full, so atoms tend to lose electrons and have low electronegativity. On the right (nonmetals), valence shells are more than half full, so atoms tend to gain electrons and have high electronegativity. Going up a family, fewer energy levels and less shielding mean stronger nuclear attraction during bonding.

Fluorine is the most electronegative element. Francium is the least. Noble gases are not reported to have electronegativity because they are often unstable.

Electronegativity increases toward the right and toward the top.
